## [PTLA01] Nascimento, Duarte & Mendes 2018 — Portuguese treatment systems differ across European and Brazilian Portuguese

**Source**  
Maria Fernanda Bacelar do Nascimento, Eugênia Duarte & Amália Mendes, `Sobre formas de tratamento no Português Europeu e Brasileiro`, *Diadorim* 20 (2018), 245–262.  
DOI: 10.35520/diadorim.2018.v20n0a23276  
https://revistas.ufrj.br/index.php/diadorim/article/view/23276

**Type**  
Published comparative sociolinguistic study.

**Supports**

- European and Brazilian Portuguese do not share one simple `tu / você` distribution.
- In European Portuguese, `tu` and `você` participate in a more complex treatment system with numerous nominal alternatives.
- In Brazilian Portuguese, `tu` and `você` are not in simple complementary distribution; in some regions they coexist as variants.
- European Portuguese frequently avoids explicit `você` through nominal forms or null subjects in relevant contexts.

**Does not support**

- `Portugal → tu`;
- `Brazil → você`;
- one country-wide treatment realization;
- marketing effectiveness claims.

## [PTLA02] Lara & Guilherme 2018 — explicit `você` vs null-subject + 3SG in European Portuguese

**Source**  
Víctor Lara & Ana Guilherme, `The politeness of você in European Portuguese`, *Studies in Hispanic and Lusophone Linguistics* 11(2), 2018, 337–366.  
DOI: 10.1515/SHLL-2018-0012  
https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/shll-2018-0012/html

**Type**  
Published corpus/pragmatics study.

**Supports**

- Current European Portuguese `você` is pragmatically polyvalent rather than one stable formal/informal marker.
- The paper reports decreasing use and marginalization of explicit `você` across the examined historical data.
- Null subject + 3SG emerges as an unmarked politeness strategy in current European Portuguese in the authors' analysis.
- Explicit `você` and null-3SG treatment should not be assumed pragmatically equivalent.

**Does not support**

- banning explicit `você` in Portugal;
- null-3SG as a universal Portuguese default;
- marketing-lift claims;
- a Portugal-wide organization style rule.

## [PTLA03] Roque & Pinto 2023 — European Portuguese treatment forms and third person without explicit subject

**Source**  
Renato Roque & Alexandra Guedes Pinto, `Formas de tratamento no português europeu: algumas considerações sobre formas neutras e não neutras`, *Redis: Revista de Estudos do Discurso* 13.  
DOI: 10.21747/21833958/red13a8  
https://ojs.letras.up.pt/index.php/re/article/view/13245

**Type**  
Published discourse/pragmatics study with exploratory contemporary data.

**Supports**

- European Portuguese treatment forms are complex, graded, subtle, and rapidly varying.
- The paper treats `você` as problematic in some contexts and explicitly relates it to third-person realization without an overt subject.
- Null third-person treatment has independent pragmatic potential rather than being only subject omission.

**Does not support**

- a universal prestige hierarchy;
- one deterministic `tu / você / senhor` scale;
- a country lookup.

## [PTLA04] Souza & Chaves 2015 — variable agreement with `tu` in Florianópolis

**Source**  
Christiane Nunes de Souza & Raquel Gomes Chaves, `A avaliação da concordância verbal com o pronome tu em Florianópolis`, *Working Papers em Linguística* 16(1), 2015, 170–199.  
DOI: 10.5007/1984-8420.2015v16n1p170

**Type**  
Brazilian sociolinguistic evaluation study.

**Supports**

- Florianópolis exhibits variable agreement with `tu`, including patterns such as `tu falas ~ tu fala` and `tu falaste ~ tu falou`.
- In the tested sample, non-marked agreement received positive/neutral evaluation consistent with changing local usage.
- `tu` therefore does not mechanically imply overt 2SG verbal morphology in every Brazilian variety/community.

**Does not support**

- `tu + 3SG-shaped agreement` as valid everywhere in Portuguese;
- intentional use in every brand voice;
- a Brazil-wide rule.

## [PTLA05] Scherre, Andrade & Catão 2021 — multiple Brazilian `tu / você` subsystems

**Source**  
Maria Marta Pereira Scherre, Carolina Queiroz Andrade & Rafael de Castro Catão, `Por onde transitam o tu e o você no Nordeste?`, *Revista de Letras* 1(40), 2021.  
DOI: 10.36517/2525-3468.rdl.v1i40.2021.71460  
https://periodicos.ufc.br/revletras/article/view/71460

**Type**  
Published synthesis of Brazilian sociolinguistic studies.

**Supports**

- Brazilian second-person subject realization includes multiple subsystems rather than one national `tu` or `você` norm.
- The synthesis distinguishes `você/cê/ocê`, `tu` without overt agreement, and `tu` with agreement across regional research.
- A country label is insufficient to infer a specific treatment system.

**Does not support**

- a region-to-pronoun lookup for marketing;
- static population preference;
- one correct subsystem for all channels.

## [PTLA06] Lopes & Cavalcante 2011 — rise of `você` subject with retention of clitic `te`

**Source**  
Célia Regina dos Santos Lopes & Silvia Regina de Oliveira Cavalcante, `A cronologia do voceamento no português brasileiro: expansão de você-sujeito e retenção do clítico-te`, *Lingüística* 25 (2011), 30–65.  
https://www.mundoalfal.org/sites/default/files/revista/25_linguistica_030_065.pdf

**Type**  
Historical sociolinguistic study using personal letters from Rio de Janeiro.

**Supports**

- Expansion of `você` in subject position did not mechanically replace the second-person clitic `te`.
- Portuguese second-person discourse reference can therefore compose forms from historically distinct paradigms.
- `você-subject + te` is not intrinsically evidence of accidental corruption.

**Does not support**

- `você → te` as a universal rule;
- current national frequency;
- brand preference.

## [PTLA07] Schwenter et al. 2018 — `te` vs tonic `você` as 2SG direct-object realizations in Brazilian Portuguese

**Source**  
Scott A. Schwenter, Mark Hoff, Kendra V. Dickinson, Justin Bland & Luana Lamberti, `Experimental evidence for 2SG direct object pronoun preferences in Brazilian Portuguese`, *Revista Linguíʃtica* 14(2), 2018, 259–290.  
DOI: 10.31513/linguistica.2018.v14n2a17608

**Type**  
Experimental Brazilian Portuguese study.

**Supports**

- Both clitic `te` and tonic `você` can realize a second-person singular direct object in Brazilian Portuguese.
- Subject-pronoun preference and discourse contrast condition the choice.
- The study explicitly notes that mixed-source pronominal combinations are ordinary for many Brazilian speakers.

**Does not support**

- `te` as universally preferred;
- one Brazilian national paradigm;
- transfer to European/African Portuguese without revalidation.

## [PTLA08] Gutiérrez Maté 2024 — `você + 2SG` in Cabinda Portuguese

**Source**  
Miguel Gutiérrez Maté, `Você + 2SG no Português de Cabinda (Angola): dois efeitos do contato linguístico`, *LaborHistórico* 10(2), 2024.  
DOI: 10.24206/lh.v10i2.64315  
https://www.revistas.ufrj.br/index.php/lh/article/view/64315

**Type**  
Published sociolinguistic/contact-linguistic study.

**Supports**

- In the studied Cabinda Portuguese system, `você` is widespread and can combine with forms derived historically from the `tu` paradigm.
- The combinations include object clitics, possessives, and to a smaller extent 2SG verbal endings.
- A pan-Portuguese `você → complete 3SG paradigm` normalization is therefore empirically false.

**Does not support**

- exporting Cabinda forms to Angola generally;
- exporting them to Brazil/Portugal/Mozambique;
- using contact-language explanation as a marketing rule.

## [PTLA09] Pires 2022 — `você` has multiple pragmatic values in Angolan Portuguese

**Source**  
Queneth Pires, `Sobre as formas de tratamento no português em Angola: o caso de você`, *Linguística: Revista de Estudos Linguísticos da Universidade do Porto*, 2022.  
https://ojs.letras.up.pt/index.php/EL/article/view/12786

**Type**  
Angolan Portuguese sociolinguistic/pragmatic study based on questionnaire data.

**Supports**

- `você` in the studied Angolan data receives multiple discourse/pragmatic values depending on context and relation.
- The paper identifies uses including intimacy, respect/affect, superiority, inferiority, distancing, interpolated, and depreciative values.
- `você` cannot safely be reduced to one global formality label across Portuguese varieties.

**Does not support**

- an Angola-wide deterministic treatment scale;
- one correct brand form;
- a hierarchy resolver.
