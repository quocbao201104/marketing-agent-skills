## [ESLA01] RAE / ASALE — `voseo`, pronominal/verbal combinations and regional paradigms

**Source**  
Real Academia Española & Asociación de Academias de la Lengua Española — `voseo`, *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas*, 2.ª ed.  
https://www.rae.es/dpd/voseo

**Evidence type**  
Authoritative pan-Hispanic linguistic reference.

**Supports**

- American voseo may affect pronominal forms, verbal forms, or both.
- Pronominal voseo uses `vos` as tonic subject / prepositional form while ordinary American voseo retains tuteante clitic and possessive forms such as `te`, `tu`, and `tuyo`.
- Voseante verb paradigms vary by tense, geography, and social distribution.
- Legitimate configurations include verbal-only voseo with subject `tú`, pronominal-only voseo with a tuteante verb, and fully voseante combinations.
- Rioplatense, Chilean, Andean, Colombian, Central-American, and other voseo patterns are not one uniform morphology.
- Rioplatense imperative forms such as `cantá / comé / viví` illustrate that address-system repair can require verb-form changes rather than pronoun-only substitution.

**Does not support**

- a universal recommendation that brands in voseante regions should use `vos`;
- a country-to-form lookup table;
- marketing-effect or conversion claims;
- one stable prestige hierarchy across every Spanish-speaking community;
- the complete runtime contract proposed by `ES-LANG-ADDR-01`.

## [ESLA02] RAE / ASALE — `vos` as a treatment form

**Source**  
Real Academia Española & Asociación de Academias de la Lengua Española — `vos`, *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas*, 2.ª ed.  
https://www.rae.es/dpd/vos

**Evidence type**  
Authoritative pan-Hispanic linguistic reference.

**Supports**

- `vos` is an established second-person singular tonic pronoun in contemporary Spanish varieties.
- In Argentina and Paraguay it is an informal/familiar treatment form.
- In some systems where `vos` and `tú` coexist, their social values need not be identical; `tú` can occupy an intermediate treatment role.

**Does not support**

- `vos = informal` as a complete pan-Hispanic rule;
- inference of the correct brand voice from nationality or country;
- one universal relationship scale that applies to `tú / vos / usted` everywhere.

## [ESLA03] Chilean treatment research — mixed pronominal and verbal voseo

**Source**  
Research article: `“Querido compadre; no podrá Ud. imajinarse…” Tratamientos pronominales y nominales en cartas personales de la pampa salitrera`, with a modern Chilean-treatment overview.  
https://www.scielo.cl/article_plus.php?lng=es&pid=S0718-93032022000100415&tlng=es

**Evidence type**  
Peer-reviewed Spanish-language sociolinguistic / historical-pragmatic research, with a literature-grounded description of modern Chilean address systems.

**Supports**

- Contemporary Chilean Spanish includes `tú`, `vos`, and `usted` in the second-person singular system.
- Documented mixed configurations include verbal voseo with `tú` (`tú + flexión voseante`) and pronominal voseo with tuteante verbal morphology (`vos + flexión tuteante`).
- Apparent pronoun/verb mismatch can therefore be an attested local system rather than accidental inconsistency.

**Does not support**

- all Chilean speakers using the same configuration;
- a rule that brands targeting Chile should adopt mixed voseo;
- normalization of every Chilean text toward any one pattern;
- a causal marketing effect.

## [ESLA04] Bogotá treatment research — social meanings of `tú` and `usted`

**Source**  
Giovani López López & Sandra Patricia Martínez Franco (2022), `Los significados sociales de las formas de tratamiento ‘tú’ y ‘usted’ en Bogotá, Colombia y sus implicaciones en la enseñanza de español como lengua extranjera`, *Revista Signos*, 55(108), 417–450.  
https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0718-09342022000100417&script=sci_arttext

DOI: https://doi.org/10.4067/S0718-09342022000100417

**Evidence type**  
Peer-reviewed ethnographic sociolinguistic study.

**Supports**

- The fixed generalization `tú = informal/close` and `usted = formal/distant` does not adequately describe all usage in the studied Bogotá group.
- Treatment forms can carry context-sensitive social, affective, and interactional meanings.
- A treatment form cannot safely be reverse-read as one universal relationship label.

**Does not support**

- a Colombia-wide treatment rule;
- current prevalence outside the studied population;
- that a marketing writer should switch forms to create a desired stance without first-party or task-specific authority;
- conversion or persuasion effects.

## [ESLA05] Academia Canaria de la Lengua — `ustedes` and plural treatment in Canarias

**Source**  
Academia Canaria de la Lengua (2026), `¿Qué diferencias existen entre el uso de los pronombres en el español de Canarias y en el español peninsular?`  
https://www.academiacanarialengua.org/consultas/2026/02/uso-pronombres/

**Evidence type**  
Current regional language-academy guidance.

**Supports**

- In the common Canarian pattern described by the Academy, `ustedes` and related verbal forms are used broadly for second-person plural address both among equals and in respectful treatment.
- The central/northern peninsular familiar-plural `vosotros` pattern therefore cannot be generalized to all varieties inside Spain.
- `ustedes` does not by itself prove formal plural relationship.

**Does not support**

- geography alone being sufficient to choose a form for a particular brand or community;
- every speaker in every Canary Island using exactly the same system;
- a Spain-versus-Latin-America binary architecture.

## [ESLA06] Rodríguez Prieto & Quintanilla 2025 — treatment forms in Salvadoran advertising

**Source**  
Juan Pablo Rodríguez Prieto & Alex JR Quintanilla (2025), `De “usted” a “tú”: el tuteo como pronombre de cortesía en el español salvadoreño`, *Hispania*, 108(2), 223–238.  
https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=10230371

**Evidence type**  
Recent empirical Spanish-language research on speaker attitudes and actual advertising usage.

**Supports**

- In the studied Salvadoran advertising contexts, treatment realization can diverge from familiar spontaneous-speech norms.
- `tú` was favored across the advertising domains examined in the study, while more formal professional-service contexts showed a more balanced distribution between `tú` and `usted`.
- The social/pragmatic value of `tú` in Salvadoran Spanish cannot be assumed to match its value in every other Spanish variety.
- Everyday local conversational norm is therefore not a sufficient marketing-realization rule.

**Does not support**

- `tú` as a universal Salvadoran advertising rule;
- `tú` as better-converting copy;
- `usted` as required for professional services;
- transfer of the measured distribution to other countries, periods, channels, or categories without evidence.

## [ESLA07] Head Start — organization- and channel-specific Spanish treatment policy

**Source**  
HeadStart.gov — `Guía de estilo para traducciones al español`.  
https://headstart.gov/es/cultura-e-idioma/articulo/guia-de-estilo-para-traducciones-al-espanol

**Evidence type**  
Authoritative first-party organizational translation/style policy.

**Supports**

- Head Start explicitly requires formal `usted` for Spanish content on its primary site / communications and informal `tú` specifically for Spanish social media.
- One organization can legitimately resolve Spanish treatment differently by communication surface.
- Current first-party organization/channel policy can therefore be authoritative for the artifact dimensions it actually controls.

**Does not support**

- US Spanish speakers generally preferring `usted` on websites and `tú` on social media;
- a cross-organization channel rule;
- a marketing-effect claim.

## [ESLA08] Mozilla localization style — es-CL and es-ES treatment policies

**Sources**  
Mozilla localization style guide — Spanish, Chile (`es-CL`).  
https://mozilla-l10n.github.io/styleguides/es-CL/

Mozilla localization style guide — Spanish, Spain (`es-ES`).  
https://mozilla-l10n.github.io/styleguides/es-ES/index.html

**Evidence type**  
First-party product/localization style policy.

**Supports**

- Mozilla `es-CL` explicitly resolves a relatively informal second-person `tú` voice for its localization context.
- Mozilla `es-ES` explicitly distinguishes product localization and web content: product strings use formal treatment where possible, while web content uses informal treatment.
- An approved organization/locale/surface policy can resolve treatment independently of a broad market stereotype.
- The same organization and language family need not use one treatment form across every product surface.

**Does not support**

- Chile-wide preference for `tú`;
- Spain-wide preference for formal product language;
- copying Mozilla's treatment policy into unrelated brands;
- a universal `product = formal / web = informal` rule.

## [ESLA09] Microsoft Spanish UI guidance — address-neutral action labels as a surface-local option

**Source**  
Microsoft Learn — `Guía de estilo de diseño y texto de la UI de Windows Admin Center` (Spanish-language page).  
https://learn.microsoft.com/es-es/windows-server/manage/windows-admin-center/extend/guides/ui-text-style-guide

**Evidence type**  
First-party product/UI writing guidance.

**Supports**

- Spanish UI labels can naturally use concise action verbs such as `Crear`, `Eliminar`, and `Agregar` without committing the label itself to `tú / vos / usted`.
- Some surfaces therefore permit natural address-neutral realization.

**Does not support**

- infinitives as a universal Spanish marketing CTA strategy;
- avoiding treatment in explanatory or conversational copy where second-person relation is part of the communication job;
- a rule that UI content never activates treatment-sensitive reasoning.

## [ESLA10] FundéuRAE — infinitive with imperative value for the public in general

**Source**  
FundéuRAE — `imperativo e infinitivo` (2014).  
https://www.fundeu.es/consulta/imperativo-e-infinitivo-334/

**Evidence type**  
Authoritative language-use guidance.

**Supports**

- Infinitive forms can legitimately have imperative value in certain constructions, including instructions addressed to the public in general such as `No tocar`.
- Spanish has some natural constructions that avoid choosing a specific second-person treatment form.

**Does not support**

- replacing direct-address imperatives with infinitives in all marketing or support copy;
- a universal neutral-Spanish fallback;
- rewriting an artifact impersonally when doing so would materially change its communication job.

## [ESLA11] RAE — `usted / ustedes` and third-person verbal morphology

**Sources**  
Real Academia Española, *Glosario de términos gramaticales*, pronoun tables.  
https://www.rae.es/gtg/docs/GTG_tablas_web.pdf

RAE/ASALE conjugation tables, e.g. `gestionar`.  
https://dle.rae.es/gestionar

**Evidence type**  
Authoritative grammatical reference.

**Supports**

- `usted / ustedes` are second-person discourse forms whose verbal agreement uses morphology shared with third-person singular/plural forms.
- Surface verbal morphology may therefore fail to uniquely identify whether the referent is the addressee or a third party when surrounding context does not disambiguate.

**Does not support**

- a claim that `usted / ustedes` are generally unclear;
- mandatory overt pronouns in every occurrence;
- one universal repair strategy for ambiguity;
- a new Spanish reference-resolution owner.

## Cross-source synthesis boundaries


## Evidence still missing or intentionally deferred

The current ledger is sufficient for the bounded freeze candidate but is not a complete Spanish sociolinguistic bibliography.

Before or during independent review, stronger evidence may still be useful for:

- discourse-driven treatment switching within the same interaction, especially current non-pedagogical corpora;
- plural-treatment variation in Andalusian varieties if that variation becomes necessary to an eval or runtime claim;
- current scoped evidence from additional voseante markets where a concrete task exposes a failure not already represented by ESLA01–ESLA06;
- treatment-sensitive possessive/reference ambiguity beyond the general `usted / ustedes` agreement fact, if the runtime contribution would prescribe a specific repair;
- LLM-specific Spanish treatment-system error rates, but only as runtime-risk evidence, never as proof of the linguistic mechanism;
- marketing outcome experiments, if future work asks whether a treatment choice changes behavior rather than merely whether the wording preserves resolved state.

Absence of those sources is not permission to generalize from the current evidence.
