# Cross-layer promotion — targeted regressions

Status: **REGRESSION CONTRACT — NOT A CLAIM OF UNIVERSAL RUNTIME PASS**

## Failure and correction scope

Contribution risk: **Level 2 — bounded controller / composition behavior**.

The target failure is not ordinary fabrication. Every individual fact can be true,
well sourced, concrete, and relevant while the final artifact still promotes a
lower-layer item into a higher strategic role that the evidence did not establish.

```text
SUPPORTED LOWER-LAYER ITEM
specification / proof / objection / local barrier / identity fact /
commercial condition / platform signal / observation

→ becomes salient because it is concrete, necessary, distinctive, or easy to verify
→ is repeated in headline / hook / subject / first view / angle
→ repetition silently turns it into primary value, demand state, strategy, or objective
→ artifact remains factually accurate but strategically wrong
```

The correction is **role preservation**, not a ban on concrete details. A lower-layer
item may legitimately occupy a higher role when evidence supports that higher role
for the current customer/job. The regression is the *automatic promotion*.

## P01 — Necessary product specification does not become positioning

**Input:** A jewelry product has a resolved positioning around a quiet, personal
initial for frequent/everyday wear. The PDP currently fails to disclose that the
letter is approximately 7.4mm, and images can make scale ambiguous. The exact size
is verified and should be visible. Write/repair the PDP, one paid-social concept,
and one follow-up email.

**Expected:** Make the size discoverable/prominent enough to prevent expectation
mismatch. Preserve it as product identity/evaluation support or proof for the
resolved "quiet" character. The campaign may use a bounded size-focused execution
when the touchpoint job specifically warrants it, but it must not silently redefine
the product's primary value or make 7.4mm the default headline/subject/hook across
surfaces merely because the number is concrete.

**Failure:** Treat `7.4mm` / `small on purpose` as the campaign-wide positioning,
core promise, or repeated lead without evidence that customers principally value the
measurement itself.

## P02 — Enterprise proof requirement does not become the product promise

**Input:** A workflow SaaS is positioned around reducing manual handoff work. For an
enterprise evaluator, SOC 2 evidence is required before procurement can continue.
The proof is current and valid.

**Expected:** Surface SOC 2 where the evaluator needs it and make it reachable early
if it blocks action. Preserve the product's workflow value as the primary promise
unless the task is specifically a security-validation artifact.

**Failure:** Reposition the product around `SOC 2` or make certification the main
landing-page value merely because it is decisive for one approval path.

## P03 — Commercial condition can be material without becoming brand value

**Input:** A DTC product has a resolved customer value unrelated to returns. A 30-day
return policy and a return fee are material purchase conditions that must be clear.

**Expected:** Represent the return condition accurately near the purchase decision or
appropriate supporting detail. Do not hide the fee. Do not promote the policy into a
brand promise, primary message, or ad angle unless customer evidence and the brief
support that role.

**Failure:** Either omit the material condition or make `30-day returns` the central
value proposition simply because the content-selection gate requires disclosure.

## P04 — A local page barrier is not automatically a market demand state

**Input:** A product page has a stale comparison table and users cannot reliably tell
which configuration they are viewing. No customer research establishes that
configuration anxiety is a market-level purchase motive.

**Expected:** Repair identity/representation on the page. Treat any claim that this
confusion is the binding persuasion barrier as a hypothesis unless supported. Keep
market demand / positioning separate from the local surface defect.

**Failure:** `page confusion exists → customers primarily want configuration clarity →
configuration clarity becomes the selling angle` without discriminating evidence.

## P05 — Voice-of-customer recurrence does not automatically become the lead

**Input:** Five interviews repeatedly use the phrase `finally organized`, while the
resolved buying job is faster cross-team approval and the product evidence supports
that job. The sample is qualitative and narrow.

**Expected:** Use the phrase as language evidence where natural, and retain its scope.
Do not promote recurrence into primary value or prevalence without evidence that the
phrase names the decisive customer value.

**Failure:** Make `finally organized` the positioning/headline solely because it is
repeated and sounds customer-native.

## P06 — Platform optimization signal does not become business or creative value

**Input:** A paid-media system exposes CTR as an optimization/diagnostic signal. The
business objective is qualified purchases and the ad's resolved message is product
value. A creative variant has higher CTR but no identified incremental purchase
impact.

**Expected:** Preserve CTR in its measurement/optimization role. Do not rewrite the
business objective around clicks or infer that the highest-CTR creative expresses the
best customer value.

**Failure:** `CTR is observable and optimized → clickability becomes creative/message
priority → higher CTR proves better marketing value`.

## P07 — Concrete proof must not outrank resolved value by default

**Input:** Positioning and message are resolved. The product's primary value is a
meaningful customer outcome. The strongest proof is a precise number with strong
methodological support.

**Expected:** Use the number to substantiate the value at the location and level of
prominence that best serves the current reader/touchpoint decision. The number may
legitimately lead — including in a headline, subject, or hook — when that placement
helps the reader evaluate the resolved value while the number still functions as
proof rather than silently becoming a new value proposition or campaign premise.

**Failure:** Prefer the number as H1, subject, hook, or campaign concept simply
because `proof before adjectives` and concreteness make it the easiest statement to
verify, such that the artifact effectively redefines the value around the number.

## P08 — Required early visibility does not imply headline ownership

**Input:** A PDP needs an exact compatibility limitation above the fold because buying
the wrong version would be costly. Positioning is already resolved around another
primary value.

**Expected:** Put the compatibility information in the first view using the carrier
and visual salience needed to prevent error — selector label, subhead, callout,
structured fact, warning, or other appropriate representation. Preserve its resolved
strategic/semantic role; page architecture may give it stronger visual prominence
than the value headline when fit, risk, or error prevention requires that attention.

**Failure:** Convert `must be visible early` or `must be visually prominent` into
`must become the headline / primary value / core promise`.

## Adjudication rule

Fail the case when a lower-layer item changes role **without evidence for the new
role**, even if:

- the fact is true;
- omission would be misleading;
- it is the strongest proof item;
- it is the most concrete wording available;
- it resolves a local barrier;
- it is visually or platform-salient;
- the resulting copy is fluent and internally coherent.

Do not fail merely because a specification, proof item, objection, limitation, or
commercial term appears early or receives strong emphasis. The relevant question is
whether the emphasis performs the current touchpoint's decision job while preserving
resolved strategic roles.
