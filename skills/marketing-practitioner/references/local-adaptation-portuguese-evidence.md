## PTLA01 — Nascimento, Duarte & Mendes 2018

**Source**  
Maria Fernanda Bacelar do Nascimento, Eugênia Duarte & Amália Mendes, `Sobre formas de tratamento no Português Europeu e Brasileiro`, *Diadorim* 20 (2018), 245–262.  
DOI: `10.35520/diadorim.2018.v20n0a23276`  
https://revistas.ufrj.br/index.php/diadorim/article/view/23276

**Type**  
Published comparative sociolinguistic study.

**Runtime support**

- European and Brazilian Portuguese do not share one simple `tu / você` distribution.
- European Portuguese includes numerous nominal alternatives and can avoid explicit `você` in relevant contexts.
- Brazilian `tu` and `você` are not one national complementary pair.

**Does not establish**

- `Portugal → tu`;
- `Brazil → você`;
- country-wide treatment rules;
- marketing effectiveness.

**Runtime role**  
Boundary evidence against country/pronoun simplification. Not sufficient by itself for promotion.

## PTLA02 — Lara & Guilherme 2018

**Source**  
Víctor Lara & Ana Guilherme, `The politeness of você in European Portuguese`, *Studies in Hispanic and Lusophone Linguistics* 11(2), 2018, 337–366.  
DOI: `10.1515/SHLL-2018-0012`

**Type**  
Published corpus/pragmatics study.

**Runtime support**

- European Portuguese explicit `você` is pragmatically polyvalent.
- The examined historical evidence shows declining/marginalized explicit use.
- Null subject + 3SG is analyzed as an unmarked politeness strategy in current European Portuguese.
- Explicit `você` and null-3SG treatment must not be assumed pragmatically equivalent.

**Does not establish**

- a ban on explicit `você`;
- null-3SG as a universal Portuguese default;
- Portugal-wide organization policy;
- marketing lift.

**Runtime role**  
Primary mechanism evidence for explicit-vs-null treatment realization.

## PTLA04 — Souza & Chaves 2015

**Source**  
Christiane Nunes de Souza & Raquel Gomes Chaves, `A avaliação da concordância verbal com o pronome tu em Florianópolis`, *Working Papers em Linguística* 16(1), 2015, 170–199.  
DOI: `10.5007/1984-8420.2015v16n1p170`

**Type**  
Scoped Brazilian sociolinguistic evaluation study.

**Runtime support**

- Florianópolis shows variable agreement with `tu`, including `tu falas ~ tu fala` and `tu falaste ~ tu falou` patterns.
- In the tested sample, non-marked agreement received positive/neutral evaluation consistent with scoped local usage.
- Visible `tu` therefore does not mechanically force overt 2SG verbal agreement everywhere.

**Does not establish**

- `tu +` 3SG-shaped agreement as valid everywhere;
- intentional use in every brand voice;
- a Brazil-wide rule.

**Runtime role**  
Primary mechanism evidence against `tu → overt 2SG agreement` normalization.

## PTLA06 — Lopes & Cavalcante 2011

**Source**  
Célia Regina dos Santos Lopes & Silvia Regina de Oliveira Cavalcante, `A cronologia do voceamento no português brasileiro: expansão de você-sujeito e retenção do clítico-te`, *Lingüística* 25 (2011), 30–65.

**Type**  
Historical sociolinguistic study using personal letters from Rio de Janeiro.

**Runtime support**

- Expansion of `você` in subject position did not mechanically replace clitic `te`.
- Portuguese second-person discourse reference can compose forms from historically distinct paradigms.
- `você` subject + `te` is not intrinsically evidence of accidental corruption.

**Does not establish**

- `você → te` as a universal rule;
- current national frequency;
- brand preference.

**Runtime role**  
Primary historical evidence against full-paradigm normalization from visible `você`.

## PTLA07 — Schwenter et al. 2018

**Source**  
Scott A. Schwenter, Mark Hoff, Kendra V. Dickinson, Justin Bland & Luana Lamberti, `Experimental evidence for 2SG direct object pronoun preferences in Brazilian Portuguese`, *Revista Linguíʃtica* 14(2), 2018, 259–290.  
DOI: `10.31513/linguistica.2018.v14n2a17608`

**Type**  
Experimental Brazilian Portuguese study.

**Runtime support**

- Both clitic `te` and tonic `você` can realize a 2SG direct object in Brazilian Portuguese.
- Dialectal subject-pronoun preference and discourse contrast condition the choice.
- Mixed-source pronominal combinations are ordinary for many speakers in the tested scope.

**Does not establish**

- universal `te` preference;
- one Brazilian national paradigm;
- transfer to European/African Portuguese without revalidation.

**Runtime role**  
Contemporary support for Mechanism A.

## PTLA08 — Gutiérrez Maté 2024

**Source**  
Miguel Gutiérrez Maté, `Você + 2SG no Português de Cabinda (Angola): dois efeitos do contato linguístico`, *LaborHistórico* 10(2), 2024.  
DOI: `10.24206/lh.v10i2.64315`

**Type**  
Published sociolinguistic/contact-linguistic study.

**Runtime support**

- In the studied Cabinda Portuguese system, `você` can combine with forms derived historically from the `tu` paradigm.
- These include object clitics, possessives, and to a smaller extent 2SG verbal endings.
- A pan-Portuguese `você → complete 3SG paradigm` normalization is empirically false.

**Does not establish**

- Cabinda forms for Angola generally;
- transfer to Brazil, Portugal, or Mozambique;
- a contact-language marketing rule.

**Runtime role**  
Strongest falsification of a universal `você → full 3SG paradigm` rule.
