## [ESLA01] RAE / ASALE — `voseo`, pronominal/verbal combinations and regional paradigms

**Source:** Real Academia Española & Asociación de Academias de la Lengua Española, `voseo`, *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas*, 2.ª ed.

**URL:** https://www.rae.es/dpd/voseo

**Type:** authoritative pan-Hispanic linguistic reference.

**Supports:**

- American voseo may affect pronominal forms, verbal forms, or both.
- Pronominal voseo uses `vos` as tonic subject / prepositional form while ordinary American voseo retains forms such as `te`, `tu`, and `tuyo`.
- Voseante verb paradigms vary by tense, geography, and social distribution.
- Legitimate configurations include verbal-only voseo with subject `tú`, pronominal-only voseo with a tuteante verb, and fully voseante combinations.
- Rioplatense imperative forms such as `cantá / comé / viví` show why pronoun-only substitution can be insufficient.

**Does not establish:**

- that brands in voseante regions should use `vos`;
- a country-to-form lookup table;
- marketing-effect or conversion claims;
- one stable prestige hierarchy across all Spanish-speaking communities.

## [ESLA02] RAE / ASALE — `vos` as a treatment form

**Source:** Real Academia Española & Asociación de Academias de la Lengua Española, `vos`, *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas*, 2.ª ed.

**URL:** https://www.rae.es/dpd/vos

**Type:** authoritative pan-Hispanic linguistic reference.

**Supports:**

- `vos` is an established second-person singular tonic pronoun in contemporary Spanish varieties.
- In Argentina and Paraguay it is an informal/familiar treatment form.
- In some systems where `vos` and `tú` coexist, their social values need not be identical.

**Does not establish:**

- `vos = informal` as a complete pan-Hispanic rule;
- the correct brand voice from nationality or country;
- one universal relationship scale for `tú / vos / usted`.

## [ESLA03] Chilean treatment research — mixed pronominal and verbal voseo

**Source:** `“Querido compadre; no podrá Ud. imajinarse…” Tratamientos pronominales y nominales en cartas personales de la pampa salitrera`, with a modern Chilean-treatment overview.

**URL:** https://www.scielo.cl/article_plus.php?lng=es&pid=S0718-93032022000100415&tlng=es

**Type:** peer-reviewed Spanish-language sociolinguistic / historical-pragmatic research with literature-grounded description of modern Chilean address systems.

**Supports:**

- Contemporary Chilean Spanish includes `tú`, `vos`, and `usted` in the second-person singular system.
- Documented mixed configurations include `tú +` voseante verbal morphology and `vos +` tuteante verbal morphology.
- Apparent pronoun/verb mismatch can therefore be an attested local system rather than accidental inconsistency.

**Does not establish:**

- that all Chilean speakers use the same configuration;
- that brands targeting Chile should adopt mixed voseo;
- normalization toward one Chilean pattern;
- a causal marketing effect.

## [ESLA04] Bogotá treatment research — social meanings of `tú` and `usted`

**Source:** Giovani López López & Sandra Patricia Martínez Franco (2022), `Los significados sociales de las formas de tratamiento ‘tú’ y ‘usted’ en Bogotá, Colombia y sus implicaciones en la enseñanza de español como lengua extranjera`, *Revista Signos*, 55(108), 417–450.

**URL:** https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0718-09342022000100417&script=sci_arttext

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.4067/S0718-09342022000100417

**Type:** peer-reviewed ethnographic sociolinguistic study.

**Supports:**

- `tú = informal/close` and `usted = formal/distant` do not adequately describe all usage in the studied Bogotá group.
- Treatment forms can carry context-sensitive social, affective, and interactional meanings.
- A treatment form cannot safely be reverse-read as one universal relationship label.

**Does not establish:**

- a Colombia-wide treatment rule;
- current prevalence outside the studied population;
- that a marketer should switch forms to create a desired stance without task-specific authority;
- conversion or persuasion effects.

## [ESLA05] Academia Canaria de la Lengua — `ustedes` and plural treatment in Canarias

**Source:** Academia Canaria de la Lengua (2026), `¿Qué diferencias existen entre el uso de los pronombres en el español de Canarias y en el español peninsular?`

**URL:** https://www.academiacanarialengua.org/consultas/2026/02/uso-pronombres/

**Type:** current regional language-academy guidance.

**Supports:**

- In the common Canarian pattern described by the Academy, `ustedes` and related verbal forms are used broadly for second-person plural address both among equals and in respectful treatment.
- Central/northern peninsular familiar-plural `vosotros` cannot be generalized to all varieties inside Spain.
- `ustedes` does not by itself prove a formal plural relationship.

**Does not establish:**

- geography alone being sufficient to choose a form for a brand or community;
- every Canary Island speaker using exactly the same system;
- a Spain-versus-Latin-America binary architecture.

## [ESLA06] Rodríguez Prieto & Quintanilla 2025 — treatment forms in Salvadoran advertising

**Source:** Juan Pablo Rodríguez Prieto & Alex JR Quintanilla (2025), `De “usted” a “tú”: el tuteo como pronombre de cortesía en el español salvadoreño`, *Hispania*, 108(2), 223–238.

**URL:** https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=10230371

**Type:** recent empirical Spanish-language research on speaker attitudes and actual advertising usage.

**Supports:**

- In the studied Salvadoran advertising contexts, treatment realization can diverge from familiar spontaneous-speech norms.
- `tú` was favored across the advertising domains examined, while more formal professional-service contexts showed a more balanced distribution between `tú` and `usted`.
- Everyday local conversational norm is not a sufficient marketing-realization rule.

**Does not establish:**

- `tú` as a universal Salvadoran advertising rule;
- `tú` as better-converting copy;
- `usted` as required for professional services;
- transfer to other countries, periods, channels, or categories without evidence.

## [ESLA07] Head Start — organization- and channel-specific Spanish treatment policy

**Source:** HeadStart.gov, `Guía de estilo para traducciones al español`.

**URL:** https://headstart.gov/es/cultura-e-idioma/articulo/guia-de-estilo-para-traducciones-al-espanol

**Type:** authoritative first-party organizational translation/style policy.

**Supports:**

- Head Start resolves formal `usted` for primary Spanish content / communications and informal `tú` for Spanish social media.
- One organization can legitimately resolve Spanish treatment differently by communication surface.
- Applicable current first-party organization/channel policy can be authoritative for the wording dimension it controls.

**Does not establish:**

- a general US-Spanish audience preference;
- a cross-organization channel rule;
- a marketing-effect claim.

## [ESLA08] Mozilla localization style — es-CL and es-ES treatment policies

**Sources:**

- Mozilla Spanish, Chile (`es-CL`): https://mozilla-l10n.github.io/styleguides/es-CL/
- Mozilla Spanish, Spain (`es-ES`): https://mozilla-l10n.github.io/styleguides/es-ES/index.html

**Type:** first-party product/localization style policy.

**Supports:**

- Mozilla `es-CL` explicitly resolves a relatively informal `tú` voice for its localization context.
- Mozilla `es-ES` distinguishes product localization and web content by treatment policy.
- An approved organization/locale/surface policy can resolve treatment independently of broad market stereotypes.
- The same organization and language family need not use one treatment form across every surface.

**Does not establish:**

- Chile-wide preference for `tú`;
- Spain-wide preference for a particular product/web treatment split;
- a universal `product = formal / web = informal` rule.

## [ESLA09] Microsoft Spanish UI guidance — address-neutral action labels

**Source:** Microsoft Learn, `Guía de estilo de diseño y texto de la UI de Windows Admin Center`.

**URL:** https://learn.microsoft.com/es-es/windows-server/manage/windows-admin-center/extend/guides/ui-text-style-guide

**Type:** first-party product/UI writing guidance.

**Supports:**

- Spanish UI labels can naturally use concise action verbs such as `Crear`, `Eliminar`, and `Agregar` without committing the label itself to `tú / vos / usted`.
- Some surfaces permit natural address-neutral realization.

**Does not establish:**

- infinitives as a universal Spanish marketing CTA strategy;
- avoiding treatment in explanatory/conversational copy where second-person relation is part of the communication job;
- that UI content never activates treatment-sensitive reasoning.

## [ESLA10] FundéuRAE — infinitive with imperative value for the public in general

**Source:** FundéuRAE, `imperativo e infinitivo` (2014).

**URL:** https://www.fundeu.es/consulta/imperativo-e-infinitivo-334/

**Type:** authoritative language-use guidance.

**Supports:**

- Infinitive forms can legitimately have imperative value in bounded constructions, including instructions to the public in general such as `No tocar`.
- Spanish has some natural constructions that avoid choosing a specific second-person treatment form.

**Does not establish:**

- replacing direct-address imperatives with infinitives in all marketing/support copy;
- a universal neutral-Spanish fallback;
- impersonal rewriting when that materially changes the communication job.

## [ESLA11] RAE — `usted / ustedes` and third-person verbal morphology

**Sources:**

- Real Academia Española, *Glosario de términos gramaticales*, pronoun tables: https://www.rae.es/gtg/docs/GTG_tablas_web.pdf
- RAE/ASALE conjugation tables, e.g. `gestionar`: https://dle.rae.es/gestionar

**Type:** authoritative grammatical reference.

**Supports:**

- `usted / ustedes` are second-person discourse forms whose verbal agreement uses morphology shared with third-person singular/plural forms.
- Surface verbal morphology may therefore fail to uniquely identify whether the referent is the addressee or a third party when context does not disambiguate.

**Does not establish:**

- that `usted / ustedes` are generally unclear;
- mandatory overt pronouns in every occurrence;
- one universal repair strategy for ambiguity;
- possessive `su / sus` ambiguity as an independently evidenced `ES-LANG-ADDR-01` mechanism;
- a new Spanish reference-resolution owner.
